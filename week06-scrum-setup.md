## Agile Delivery Fit

The patient intake and scheduling workflows could benefit from iterative delivery because RCHN staff from all six clinics can review early versions of the workflows and provide feedback before the system is fully completed. For example, the team could create an early scheduling workflow prototype, allow clinic staff to test it, and then adapt the design based on what they learn. This supports Agile because the team can use frequent feedback instead of waiting until the end of the project to discover problems.

One area that may require stronger advance planning is the migration and cleanup of existing patient and scheduling data. The team needs to understand the quality of the existing data and plan how it will be handled before the new system goes live so important information is not lost or transferred incorrectly.

## Sprint Planning Reflection

I selected the Scheduling Workflow Prototype, Intake Form Standardization, and Sprint Communication Update because together they create a focused Sprint 1 plan that supports the Sprint Goal. The Scheduling Workflow Prototype and Intake Form Standardization are both high-priority items and address two of the main areas RCHN needs to improve across its six clinics.

The Sprint Communication Update supports the team by creating a simple way to keep stakeholders informed and gather feedback. These three items total 23 points, which stays within the 25-point course planning limit without overloading the team.

Early in the project, I think the team needs to learn whether the proposed scheduling workflow and standardized intake fields actually work for clinic staff before moving into less important features. I intentionally deferred the Legacy Data Cleanup Review. Although data quality will be important later, it is lower priority and can be addressed in a future Sprint after the team has received feedback on the core scheduling and intake workflows.

## Scrum Master Reflection

### Obstacle and Response

One impediment that could affect progress toward the Sprint Goal is receiving conflicting feedback from staff at the six different RCHN clinics. Different locations may have different ways of handling scheduling and patient intake, which could make it difficult for the Developers to agree on a standardized workflow.

As Scrum Master, I would help by making the impediment visible, improving communication between the team and stakeholders, and helping organize discussions so the team can get the information it needs. I would not make the workflow decision for the Developers or Product Owner. Instead, I would help remove communication barriers and support the Scrum Team in finding its own solution while staying focused on the Sprint Goal.

### Preparing for Week 7

The Sprint Planning work from this week will help me build and prioritize the Product Backlog in Week 7 because I now have a better understanding of which RCHN features provide the most value and how much work the team can realistically take on at one time.

The Scheduling Workflow Prototype and Intake Form Standardization were prioritized because they directly support the main needs of the project. In Week 7, these planning decisions can help guide the creation of user stories and acceptance criteria.

Sprint Planning also showed me that important work does not always need to be completed immediately. The Product Backlog can remain ordered so lower-priority items are available for future Sprints while the team focuses first on work that best supports the current Product Goal and Sprint Goal.
