## Estimation Approach

I used a combination of parametric and bottom-up estimating. Parametric estimating works well for labor because I calculated cost using total hours multiplied by an estimated hourly rate. Bottom-up estimating also fits because the RCHN project already has defined work such as configuration, migration, testing, training, and deployment.

The main limitation is that the hourly rates and estimated hours are course estimates rather than confirmed NovaTech rates.

Assumption 1: The Project Manager will average about 12 hours per week during the 34-week schedule. If more coordination is needed across the six clinics, the total labor cost could increase.

Assumption 2: The Developer can complete most configuration and migration work within 20 weeks at about 25 hours per week. If technical problems or migration errors require extra work, development hours and project cost could increase.

## Budget Tradeoff Analysis

Before agreeing to a 10 percent budget reduction, I would review which costs could be reduced without affecting the approved project outcome, system quality, or clinic readiness.

Tradeoff or Risk 1: Reducing Developer or QA hours too much could increase the chance of defects, incomplete testing, or migration problems, which could hurt quality and delay go-live.

Tradeoff or Risk 2: Reducing training, coordination, or support resources could create readiness problems at the six clinics and increase the chance of rework or stakeholder dissatisfaction.

I would avoid reducing essential testing, migration validation, or readiness activities because those areas directly connect to the Week 9 risks and project quality.
