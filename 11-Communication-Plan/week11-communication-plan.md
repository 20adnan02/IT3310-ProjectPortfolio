## Communication Matrix

### Entry 1
**Stakeholder:** RCHN Chief Medical Officer  
**Information Needed:** Major clinical workflow changes, patient safety concerns, testing results, approvals, and readiness updates.  
**Timing / Trigger:** During workflow review, before major clinical approvals, or when a change could affect patient care.  
**Frequency:** Bi-weekly  
**Format:** Meeting  
**Owner:** Project Manager  
**Method:** Interactive  

### Entry 2
**Stakeholder:** RCHN Board of Directors  
**Information Needed:** Overall project status, major risks, budget and schedule updates, milestones, and important decisions.  
**Timing / Trigger:** At major milestones or when a significant schedule, budget, or scope change occurs.  
**Frequency:** Monthly  
**Format:** Executive summary  
**Owner:** Project Manager  
**Method:** Push  

### Entry 3
**Stakeholder:** Clinic Intake Coordinators  
**Information Needed:** Workflow changes, testing progress, training dates, readiness updates, and changes affecting daily work.  
**Timing / Trigger:** During testing and training and before workflow changes or deployment.  
**Frequency:** Weekly  
**Format:** Status email  
**Owner:** Product Manager  
**Method:** Push  

### Entry 4
**Stakeholder:** Healthcare Privacy and Regulatory Oversight Representatives  
**Information Needed:** Privacy, security, compliance requirements, data-handling concerns, and regulatory changes.  
**Timing / Trigger:** When a new privacy, security, or regulatory concern is identified.  
**Frequency:** As-needed  
**Format:** Compliance checklist  
**Owner:** Project Manager  
**Method:** Push  

### Entry 5
**Stakeholder:** RCHN IT Support and Infrastructure Team  
**Information Needed:** Technical requirements, testing results, known issues, environment readiness, deployment timing, and support needs.  
**Timing / Trigger:** During configuration and testing, before deployment, or when a technical issue affects readiness.  
**Frequency:** Bi-weekly  
**Format:** Meeting  
**Owner:** Scrum Master  
**Method:** Interactive  

### Communication Method Justification

**Entry 1 - RCHN Chief Medical Officer:** I chose Interactive communication because the CMO has high interest and high influence in the Week 4 Stakeholder Register and may need to review or approve important clinical workflow decisions. A bi-weekly meeting allows NovaTech to explain impacts, answer questions, and receive decisions or clarification before work moves forward.

**Entry 3 - Clinic Intake Coordinators:** I chose Push communication because these users need timely and consistent operational information about workflow changes, testing, and training. Their Week 4 classification was high interest and low influence, so a weekly status email keeps them informed without requiring unnecessary meetings while still giving them clear information they can act on.

## Escalation Path

### Level 1 - Scrum Master
Handles team-level communication or delivery issues that can be resolved within the NovaTech delivery team. Triggered when a communication is missed, a minor blocker appears, or a delivery issue can be corrected without changing scope, budget, or a major milestone.

**Expected Response Time:** Within 4 business hours.

### Level 2 - Project Manager
Handles issues that require client involvement or could affect schedule, quality, compliance, or multiple clinics. Triggered when the issue cannot be resolved at the team level or could affect a milestone.

**Expected Response Time:** Within 1 business day.

### Level 3 - RCHN Project Sponsor
Handles issues that require executive approval, such as major schedule changes, additional funding or staffing, major scope changes, or serious compliance concerns.

**Expected Response Time:** Within 24 hours for urgent issues.

### Escalation Scenario

If Risk 3 from my Week 9 Risk Register materialized and patient or appointment data was migrated incorrectly, the escalation would begin with the Developer and Scrum Master validating the data problem and identifying the affected records.

**Step 1:** The Developer and Scrum Master would investigate and begin correcting the problem.

**Step 2:** If the issue cannot be resolved within one business day, affects multiple clinics, or threatens testing or readiness, the Project Manager would coordinate the response and assess the schedule, quality, and resource impact.

**Step 3:** If the issue is critical, requires additional funding or staffing, or could delay the milestone or go-live, the Project Manager would escalate it to the RCHN Project Sponsor for a final decision.

## Gap Analysis

### Communication Gap

The gap I identified in my communication plan is that an urgent privacy or regulatory change could happen between normal updates without a clear immediate response trigger. This matters because a new requirement involving patient data could affect system design, testing, or readiness. To fix this gap, I would add an immediate communication trigger for the Healthcare Privacy and Regulatory Oversight Representatives so the Project Manager contacts them as soon as a serious privacy, security, or regulatory concern is identified.

### Silence as a Communication Decision

A situation where NovaTech silence could create a stakeholder problem is during testing and training before the Phase One Workflow Acceptance milestone. Even if the project is on track, Clinic Intake Coordinators might assume that training dates changed, workflow decisions are not finalized, or they are not ready for the next step. My communication plan prevents this by providing weekly status emails to the Clinic Intake Coordinators and making sure they receive timely updates during testing, training, and milestone readiness.
