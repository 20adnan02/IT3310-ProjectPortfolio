## Project Manager Role

The Project Manager focuses on planning and organizing how the project will be completed, including the schedule, scope, resources, risks, and communication. The Product Manager focuses more on the users, their needs, and making sure the product provides value. RCHN benefits from both roles because the Project Manager helps keep the project organized and realistic, while the Product Manager helps make sure the new intake and scheduling system actually works well for clinic staff and patients. The two roles would need to work closely together when deciding priorities, reviewing requirements, managing changes, and making sure the project stays within scope while still meeting user needs.

---

## Scope

### In Scope, Phase One

- Patient intake workflow standardization across all six clinics
- Appointment scheduling system replacement
- Staff training for the new system at all six locations
- Initial rollout support for 90 days post-launch

### Out of Scope, Phase One

- Patient-facing online scheduling portal
- Billing system integration with the new scheduling data
- Executive reporting dashboard for clinic leadership
- Mobile application for clinic staff

### Scope Justification

I recommend excluding the patient-facing online scheduling portal from phase one because the main goal of the project is to replace and standardize RCHN's internal patient intake and scheduling system across all six clinics. Adding a patient-facing portal would increase the project scope and require additional design, testing, security, privacy, and support work. This could increase the schedule, cost, resource needs, and overall project complexity. The portal could be considered in a future phase after the core system is successfully implemented.

## SMART Objectives

### Objective 1

By the approved phase-one go-live milestone, RCHN will implement a standardized patient intake and appointment scheduling workflow across all six clinics, verified through agreed workflow testing and acceptance criteria at each location. The final acceptance criteria will require sponsor approval.

**Connection to Product Vision:**  
This objective connects to my Week 3 product vision because clinic staff need a reliable system with more consistent intake and scheduling workflows. Standardizing the process across all six clinics should make daily work more consistent and improve appointment accuracy.

### Objective 2

By the approved phase-one go-live milestone, staff at all six RCHN clinics will complete training on the new intake and scheduling system, verified through training completion records and approved readiness criteria. The final readiness criteria will require sponsor approval.

**Connection to Product Vision:**  
This objective connects to my Week 3 product vision because staff need clear and practical workflows they can follow consistently. Training will help employees understand the new system and reduce confusion and workarounds after implementation.

## Planning Constraints

### Constraint 1

RCHN operates six clinics that currently have inconsistent patient intake and scheduling workflows.

**Effect on Planning:**  
This affects scope, schedule, resources, and quality because the project team must gather input from all six clinics and create a standardized process while still considering necessary differences between locations.

### Constraint 2

Major clinical workflow changes require approval from RCHN executive clinical leadership, including the Chief Medical Officer.

**Effect on Planning:**  
This affects the schedule, scope, and project risk because important workflow decisions may need executive review and approval before the team can finalize or implement them.

---

## Assumptions

### Assumption 1

We are assuming that staff from all six RCHN clinics will be available to participate in workflow review, testing, and training during the planned implementation period.

**If this is wrong:**  
The project schedule and resource plan may need to change, and testing, training, or rollout activities may need to be completed in phases.

### Assumption 2

We are assuming that RCHN's current technical environment can support the replacement intake and scheduling system without requiring a major infrastructure upgrade.

**If this is wrong:**  
The project scope, cost, resource requirements, technical work, and schedule may need to increase before implementation can continue.

---

## Planning Foundation Synthesis

One planning decision I made this week was to keep phase one focused on the core intake and scheduling system replacement, workflow standardization, staff training, and initial rollout support. My Week 3 product vision informed this decision because it showed that clinic staff need a reliable system, consistent workflows, accurate scheduling, and practical training. I also had to use project management judgment to keep additional features, such as the patient portal, billing integration, executive dashboard, and mobile application, out of phase one so the project does not become too complex. Before this decision is final, the executive sponsor still needs to confirm the phase-one scope, acceptance criteria, and implementation approach.
